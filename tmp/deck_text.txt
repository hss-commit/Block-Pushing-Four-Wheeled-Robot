02 · Overall Technical Roadmap
Core Strategy: Collection, Not Pushing
We adopt a "collection-type" strategy, fundamentally different from "push-type" approaches
Strategy Overview
The robot actively collects encountered blocks into an internal bin via front side brushes and a central intake, then dumps at optimal timing.
Strategy Choice
couples "search" from "transport"
Efficiency Logic
Sweep multiple blocks continuously, reduce back-and-forth trips
Key Design Decision
choosing which cluster is worth sweeping,
from which direction,
how many can be collected at once,
and when to return to dump.
Main Risks & Countermeasures
Jamming, bin-full, and dump failure are the primary risks
Must be mitigated with feedback and recovery mechanisms

Decision: stability under contact outranks agility — the tracked chassis is locked as the project baseline.
Why Stability Wins Matches
Blocks are cleared by sustained blade contact, not by single touches. A chassis that holds its heading under load converts motor power into net block displacement instead of spin.
With both robots on the field for the full 90 s, collision survival is a scoring variable, not a comfort feature.
DOWNSTREAM CONSEQUENCE
The tactic layer can assume a wall-like rigid body — this assumption is later exploited by the defensive strategy found in simulation (Part 04).

R2 — The Structural Dead Zone: Tactical Keystone
04 · Results & Discussion
EXPERIMENT 2
Fig. 4 — Exp. 2: Time-Weighted Mean Residual (TWMR) vs Push Depth Beyond the Centre Line
Crossing ≥ 0.30 m delivers blocks to y = 0.911 — inside the opponent's structural dead zone · shaded band = seed dispersion
Why a Dead Zone Exists
A one-way blade can only push away from its own body. The strip within one body-length of the far wall is therefore geometrically unreachable for the opponent — blocks placed there can never be retrieved.
Two Regimes, One Threshold
· Crossing 0.10–0.20 m: blocks stay reachable → TWMR rises to ~17
· Crossing ≥ 0.30 m: dead-zone lock-in → TWMR falls monotonically
· Full depth 0.59 m: optimum
TACTICAL RULE: never stop a push halfway — always drive blocks to the far wall.

R2 — The Structural Dead Zone: Tactical Keystone
04 · Results & Discussion
EXPERIMENT 2
Fig. 4 — Exp. 2: Time-Weighted Mean Residual (TWMR) vs Push Depth Beyond the Centre Line
Crossing ≥ 0.30 m delivers blocks to y = 0.911 — inside the opponent's structural dead zone · shaded band = seed dispersion
Why a Dead Zone Exists
A one-way blade can only push away from its own body. The strip within one body-length of the far wall is therefore geometrically unreachable for the opponent — blocks placed there can never be retrieved.
Two Regimes, One Threshold
· Crossing 0.10–0.20 m: blocks stay reachable → TWMR rises to ~17
· Crossing ≥ 0.30 m: dead-zone lock-in → TWMR falls monotonically
· Full depth 0.59 m: optimum
TACTICAL RULE: never stop a push halfway — always drive blocks to the far wall.
